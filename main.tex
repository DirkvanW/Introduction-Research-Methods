\section{Introduction}
This study analyses the linguistic diversity in Project Gutenberg's digital library collection over time, examining changes in the additions for each language in the Gutenberg Collection per year. The research aims to understand how increased internet access in non-English speaking countries may have influenced digital preservation efforts. It does this by analysing the works added year over year to the Gutenberg collection, showing the changes in addition rates of differing languages. 
\section{Related Work}
Several studies have examined linguistic diversity in digital libraries and Project Gutenberg specifically:

\autocite{whitt_diachronic_2018} explored multilingualism on the web and Project Gutenberg's early efforts at linguistic diversification, providing important historical context for understanding the evolution of digital preservation efforts.

2. Gerlach, M., \& Font-Clos, F. (2020) analysed a curated Project Gutenberg database, revealing a strong bias toward Western languages, with English comprising 81\% of all books. Their work established baseline metrics for understanding linguistic representation in digital libraries.

\section{Predicted Results}
We expect that the data will show an increase in the proportion of non-English books in Project Gutenberg's collection over time. This is based on the idea that increased internet access in non-English speaking countries leads to greater easier access and participation with Project Gutenberg.
